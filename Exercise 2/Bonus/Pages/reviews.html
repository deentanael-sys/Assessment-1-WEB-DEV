Reviews - Project Hail Mary

Humanity needs this kind of movies

Project Hail Mary is the kind of movie that reminds you why you love cinema. It's been a long time since I felt such mixed emotions while watching a film-happiness, sadness, love, hope-everything is there. I went into it completely blind. I didn't watch a trailer; I only knew that Ryan Gosling was starring and that it was directed by Phil Lord and Christopher Miller, and that alone made me believe it would be something special.

Touching, funny and captivating.

Although the way this space adventure is written and filmed keeps you enraptured with the plot and sense of adventure, it is easy to forget that Ryan Gosling spends most of his time acting alone, and he deserves much credit for his seamless performance. Apparently, no green screen was used during the filming with a set built for the spaceship interior and an animatronic or puppet used for Rocky, with SFX added to enhance it afterwards.

Fist my bump 👎👎

The film is basically what you'd expect, but it's so incisive and cleverly conceived that it keeps surprising you.

For a long time, it's mostly funny, but starting with Sandra Hüller's karaoke performance, it undergoes a tonal shift that takes the film to a whole new level.

It often strongly evokes the greatest moments of 2001 or Interstellar, but takes them further, creating suspense and heart-wrenching moments you wouldn't have expected.

The cinematography is breathtaking and will 100% warrant at least one Oscar nomination, the same goes for the soundtrack and the song selection at certain points, blending folk, country and classics together.

The science aspect is conveyed in an easy-to-understand way, yet it's also presented that it's fun to follow along and think through the story.

The absolute highlight, however, is Ryan Gosling's performance and his chemistry with Rocky, which will linger in your mind for a long time and is sure to inspire some nostalgic edits in the coming years.

Just how much you come to care for the teacher and his stone in those few hours, how much you can discover alongside them in this friendship, right up to the ultimate self-sacrifice, brings some tears to your eyes.

Admittedly, it does drag a bit in places and could have taken an even more intellectual and sophisticated direction, but given the film as a whole, all the praise and the already outstanding reviews from critics and audiences alike are more than justified.

Amaze, Amaze, Amaze.
